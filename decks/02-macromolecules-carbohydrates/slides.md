# What Are Macromolecules?

<p class="max-w-4xl -mt-2">
  <strong>Macromolecules</strong> are large, biologically important molecules involved in all
  structures and processes of cells and organisms. They are <strong>polymers</strong> — many
  monomers strung together — built through <strong>Condensation</strong> (removal of H₂O) or
  <strong>Hydrolysis</strong> (addition of H₂O) reactions.
</p>

# Monosaccharides: Building Blocks

<p class="-mt-2 mb-3 max-w-4xl text-sm">
  The monomers of carbohydrates are <strong>Monosaccharides</strong>. Two joined form a
  <strong>Disaccharide</strong>; many form <strong>Polysaccharides</strong>. The bond is the
  <strong>Glycosidic Linkage</strong> — a covalent bond between the 1st carbon of one molecule and
  the 4th carbon of another, formed via condensation.
</p>

<div class="grid grid-cols-[1.1fr_0.9fr] gap-6 items-center">
  <div>
    <h3 class="!text-[1.05rem] mb-2">Classification by Carbon Count</h3>
    <div class="grid-2 gap-3 text-sm">
      <div class="card-soft"><strong>3 — Triose</strong></div>
      <div class="card-soft"><strong>4 — Tetrose</strong></div>
      <div class="card-soft"><strong>5 — Pentose</strong><p class="text-xs mt-1">Ribose, Deoxyribose</p></div>
      <div class="card-soft"><strong>6 — Hexose</strong><p class="text-xs mt-1">Glucose, Fructose</p></div>
    </div>
    <div class="card mt-3"><h3 class="!text-base">Function</h3><p class="text-sm mt-1">Used for <strong>energy storage &amp; production</strong>.</p></div>
  </div>

# Fructose vs. Glucose

<div class="grid-2 mt-2 gap-6 items-stretch">
  <div class="card">
    <div class="flex items-center justify-center"><Carbonyl type="ketone" color="#E0556B" /></div>
    <h3>Fructose — Ketohexose</h3>
    <p class="text-sm mt-1">6-carbon skeleton with an <strong>internal</strong> carbonyl group (ketone) at
    C2. Found in fruits and honey.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="card">
    <div class="flex items-center justify-center"><Carbonyl type="aldehyde" color="#1E6FB8" /></div>
    <h3>Glucose — Aldohexose</h3>
    <p class="text-sm mt-1">6-carbon skeleton with a carbonyl group at the <strong>end</strong> of the
    skeleton (aldehyde) at C1. Primary energy source.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="card-soft mt-5 text-sm flex items-start gap-3">
  <Ic name="dna" class="text-[var(--mda-blue)] text-xl shrink-0 mt-0.5" />
  <span>Glucose &amp; Fructose are <strong>Structural Isomers</strong> — the same molecular formula
  <strong>C₆H₁₂O₆</strong>, but their atoms are arranged differently.</span>
</div>

# Glucose Isomers: α &amp; β Forms

<p class="-mt-2 mb-4 max-w-4xl text-sm">
  Glucose forms a ring when C1 bonds covalently with C5. In ring form, glucose exists as two
  <strong>stereoisomers</strong> — same formula and bonding sequence, but a different 3-D
  orientation of the hydroxyl group on the anomeric carbon (C1).
</p>

<div class="grid-2 gap-6 items-center">
  <div class="card flex items-center gap-4">
    <SugarRing color="#1E6FB8" anomer="alpha" />
    <div>
      <h3>α-D Glucose</h3>
      <p class="text-sm mt-1">The OH group at C1 points <strong>down</strong>. Forms
      <strong>digestible</strong> linkages — e.g. in Starch and Maltose.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="card flex items-center gap-4">
    <SugarRing color="#14B8A6" anomer="beta" />
    <div>
      <h3>β-D Glucose</h3>
      <p class="text-sm mt-1">The OH group at C1 points <strong>up</strong>. Forms
      <strong>indigestible</strong> linkages — e.g. in Cellulose and Cellobiose.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

# Significance of α &amp; β Glucose

<div class="grid-2 mt-3 gap-6">
  <div class="card">
    <div class="flex items-center gap-1 mb-2">
      <SugarRing color="#1E6FB8" anomer="alpha" />
      <SugarRing color="#1E6FB8" anomer="alpha" />
    </div>
    <h3>Maltose — α-linkage</h3>
    <p class="text-sm mt-1"><strong>Amylase</strong>, our digestive enzyme, can break the glycosidic
    linkage between two α-glucose molecules. Maltose (α-D glucose + α-D glucose) is
    <strong>fully digestible</strong>.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="card">
    <div class="flex items-center gap-1 mb-2">
      <SugarRing color="#14B8A6" anomer="beta" />
      <SugarRing color="#14B8A6" anomer="beta" />
    </div>
    <h3>Cellobiose — β-linkage</h3>
    <p class="text-sm mt-1">Humans <strong>cannot digest</strong> cellobiose (β-D glucose + β-D
    glucose). However, it is recommended nutritionally — it increases intestinal peristalsis,
    reducing the risk of colon cancer.</p>
  </div>
</div>

---

# Glucose in the Body

<p class="-mt-2 mb-3 max-w-4xl text-sm">Glucose is the most abundant monosaccharide and the
<strong>sole energy source for the brain, kidneys, and red blood cells</strong>. The liver, aided by
pancreatic hormones, keeps blood glucose constant.</p>

<div class="grid-2 gap-5">
  <div class="card">
    <div class="flex items-center gap-2"><div class="chip-icon"><Ic name="chevron-right" /></div><h3>Insulin</h3></div>
    <p class="text-sm mt-2">Released when glucose is <strong>high</strong>. Liver cells
    <strong>absorb</strong> glucose → lowers blood glucose. Prevents <strong>Hyperglycemia</strong>.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="card">
    <div class="flex items-center gap-2"><div class="chip-icon"><Ic name="chevron-right" /></div><h3>Glucagon</h3></div>
    <p class="text-sm mt-2">Released when glucose is <strong>low</strong>. Liver cells
    <strong>release</strong> glucose → raises blood glucose. Prevents <strong>Hypoglycemia</strong>.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="mt-4 rounded-2xl px-4 py-3 text-sm" style="background:#FDE4E1;color:#C0392B">
  <strong>Hypoglycemia</strong> (low blood glucose) and <strong>Hyperglycemia</strong> (high blood
  glucose, e.g. Diabetes Mellitus) can both lead to serious disease or death.
</div>

---

# Disaccharides

<p class="-mt-2 mb-4 max-w-4xl text-sm">Disaccharides form when two monosaccharides join via a
<strong>Glycosidic Linkage</strong>. They can be <strong>hydrolyzed</strong> — split by adding water —
back into two monosaccharides (e.g. Sucrose + H₂O → Glucose + Fructose).</p>

<div class="grid-4 gap-4 text-sm">
  <div class="card"><div class="chip-icon mb-2"><Ic name="bread-slice" /></div><h3 class="!text-base">Maltose</h3><p class="text-xs mt-1">α-D glucose + α-D glucose. "Malt sugar." Digestible by humans.</p></div>
  <div class="card"><div class="chip-icon mb-2"><Ic name="bread-slice" /></div><h3 class="!text-base">Sucrose</h3><p class="text-xs mt-1">α-D glucose + Fructose. "Table sugar." Most common dietary sugar.</p></div>
  <div class="card"><div class="chip-icon mb-2"><Ic name="oil" /></div><h3 class="!text-base">Lactose</h3><p class="text-xs mt-1">Glucose + Galactose. "Milk sugar." Some humans are lactose intolerant.</p></div>
  <div class="card"><div class="chip-icon mb-2"><Ic name="content-cut" /></div><h3 class="!text-base">Cellobiose</h3><p class="text-xs mt-1">β-D glucose + β-D glucose. Cannot be digested by humans.</p></div>
</div>

---

# Polysaccharides

<p class="-mt-2 mb-3 max-w-4xl text-sm">Polysaccharides are many monosaccharides joined by glycosidic
linkages, forming long chains (straight or branched). They are often <strong>water-insoluble</strong>
due to their many non-polar C–H bonds.</p>

<div class="grid-3 gap-4">
  <div class="card">
    <SugarChain variant="branched" color="#1E6FB8" />
    <h3 class="!text-base mt-1">Glycogen</h3>
    <p class="text-xs mt-1">Polymer of α-D glucose. <strong>Highly branched</strong> &amp; more water
    soluble. Energy store in animal cells (liver &amp; muscle). "Animal sugar."</p>
  </div>
  <div class="card">
    <SugarChain variant="straight" color="#14B8A6" />
    <h3 class="!text-base mt-1">Starch</h3>
    <p class="text-xs mt-1">Polymer of α-D glucose. Less branched, less soluble. Energy store in plant
    cells. <strong>Digestible</strong> by humans. "Plant sugar."</p>
  </div>
  <div class="card">
    <SugarChain variant="straight" color="#D9A23B" />
    <h3 class="!text-base mt-1">Cellulose</h3>
    <p class="text-xs mt-1">Polymer of β-D glucose. Straight chain, water-insoluble. Adds
    <strong>structural strength</strong> to plant cell walls. Indigestible — dietary fiber.</p>
  </div>
</div>

# Carbohydrates at a Glance

<div class="grid-2 gap-4 mt-3">
  <div class="card"><h3>Structure</h3><p class="text-sm mt-1">Formula <strong>CₙH₂ₙOₙ</strong> (1:2:1); contain an <strong>Aldehyde</strong> or <strong>Ketone</strong> group; units join via the <strong>Glycosidic Linkage</strong>.</p></div>
  <div class="card"><h3>Monomers &amp; Isomers</h3><p class="text-sm mt-1">Monosaccharides (Glucose, Fructose, Galactose). Glucose &amp; Fructose are structural isomers; α- vs β-glucose are stereoisomers.</p></div>
  <div class="card"><h3>Blood Glucose Regulation</h3><p class="text-sm mt-1"><strong>Insulin</strong> lowers blood glucose; <strong>Glucagon</strong> raises it. Hyperglycemia ▲ vs Hypoglycemia ▼.</p></div>
  <div class="card"><h3>α vs β Linkages</h3><p class="text-sm mt-1"><strong>α</strong> (Starch, Glycogen, Maltose) are digestible by amylase; <strong>β</strong> (Cellulose, Cellobiose) are indigestible but valuable fiber.</p></div>
</div>
